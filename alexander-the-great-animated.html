The chronicle

A campaign that opened at Chaeronea and closed, thirteen years later, in a sickroom in Babylon.

338 BC

Chaeronea

At eighteen, Alexander leads the Macedonian cavalry that breaks the Theban Sacred Band, securing Philip II's control of Greece.

336 BC

Accession

Philip II is assassinated at Aegae. Alexander is proclaimed king of Macedon at twenty.

335 BC

Thebes destroyed

After a revolt, Alexander razes Thebes and enslaves its survivors, a warning to the rest of Greece.

334 BC

Crossing into Asia

Alexander crosses the Hellespont and defeats a Persian force at the Granicus River, his first battle on Asian soil.

333 BC

The Gordian Knot & Issus

At Gordium he cuts, rather than unties, the legendary knot. At Issus he routs Darius III, who flees the field.

332 BC

Tyre and Egypt

A seven-month siege breaks Tyre's island fortress. Egypt submits without a fight; Alexander founds Alexandria on the coast.

331 BC

Gaugamela

Alexander's final, decisive battle against Darius III shatters the Persian royal army near modern-day Erbil.

330 BC

Persepolis and Darius's death

The Persian ceremonial capital is taken and its palace burned. Darius III is killed by his own satrap, Bessus.

327–326 BC

Into India

Alexander marries Roxana in Bactria, then crosses the Indus. At the Hydaspes he defeats King Porus in the campaign's hardest-fought battle.

326 BC

The Hyphasis mutiny

At the Hyphasis River his exhausted troops refuse to march further east. Alexander turns the army back.

323 BC

Death in Babylon

Alexander dies in Babylon after a fever, aged thirty-two. His empire is divided among his generals, the Diadochi.
